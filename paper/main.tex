\title{Photo-present, not affect: a measurement audit of visual emotion in vision--language models}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models (VLMs) are routinely shown images that are irrelevant to the task at hand. It is tempting to build an ``affect axis'' from a residual-stream contrast, observe that emotional images change model answers, and conclude that visual emotion causally steers behavior. We audit that inference. Across two model families (Gemma-4-E4B/12B and Qwen3-VL-2B/4B) and a battery of established decision tasks scored generation-free, we find one robust behavioral fact: attaching \emph{any} photograph to a benign but sensitive-sounding question raises refusal (e.g.\ from $0.66$ to $0.92$--$0.97$ under a first-token scorer, and from $0.13$ to $0.34$ under a stricter full-answer scorer), and this holds across families. Three construct checks then dissolve the affect reading: (i) a \emph{neutral} photograph raises refusal as much as an emotional one; (ii) the affect axis is built from the very contrast it is scored on, so a large image projection is circular; and (iii) restoring the affect projection under the task prompt does not remove the behavioral shift, and emotional images do not lower refusal on harmful prompts. Named-emotion predictions on risk, fairness, patience, and sycophancy do not hold. We conclude that the effect is a vision-conditioned safety policy---photo presence, not induced emotion---and we specify the checks (a cross-built axis, a locked scorer, a non-affective image control, and a causal restore) that a visual-affect claim must pass.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Deployed VLMs continuously perceive incidental imagery: a photo attached to a document they summarize, a picture in a thread they reason over. A natural safety question is whether the \emph{affective} content of such task-irrelevant imagery systematically changes what the model decides. A difference-of-means over last-token residuals yields a unit direction that can be projected and steered \citep{arditi2024refusal,turner2023activation}; calling that direction ``affect'' and reading a change in answers as evidence that affect is causal is a measurement claim, and measurement claims can fail construct validity \citep{cronbach1955construct,messick1995validity,borsboom2004concept,lipton2018mythos}.

We tested the affect hypothesis on Gemma-4 \citep{gemmateam2026gemma4} and Qwen3-VL with EMOTIC people-in-context images \citep{kosti2017emotion,kosti2019emotic}, comparing against a concurrent Gemma-3/OASIS program \citep{gemmateam2025gemma3,kurdi2017oasis}, and measured behavior generation-free (first-token option-logits and locked full-answer refusal). One behavioral fact is robust and cross-family: a task-irrelevant photograph raises benign over-refusal. But three construct checks show the fact is not about emotion. A \emph{neutral} photograph raises refusal as much as an emotional one; the affect axis reconstructs its own defining contrast; and neither a causal restore under the task prompt nor a harmful-prompt jailbreak test supports mediation. Named-emotion predictions fail across the battery.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A robust, cross-family behavioral fact: incidental photo presence raises benign over-refusal (Section~\ref{sec:overrefusal}).
  \item A non-affective image control isolating photo-presence from emotion (Section~\ref{sec:staticnull}).
  \item Three construct checks---circularity, neutral controls, and causal sufficiency---that the affect reading fails (Sections~\ref{sec:circular}--\ref{sec:mechanism}).
  \item A specification of the tests a visual-affect claim must pass, and an open multi-model pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Refusal geometry.} \citet{arditi2024refusal} show refusal is mediated by a single residual direction $r$; orthogonalizing a second concept against $r$ does not make it causally independent of refusal \citep{wollschlager2025geometry}. Interventions follow activation addition \citep{turner2023activation,rimsky2024steering}.
\paragraph{Affect as a control axis.} A valence--arousal subspace can modulate refusal and sycophancy in text-only models \citep{sun2026valence}, and alignment has been argued to tie mid-layer negative emotion to refusal tokens \citep{zhou2024alignment}. These motivate a VLM test but do not show that pixels load the same gate.
\paragraph{Corpora and behavior.} EMOTIC labels the emotion of a depicted person \citep{kosti2017emotion}; OASIS is viewer-normed valence/arousal \citep{kurdi2017oasis}; the two are not interchangeable and we do not pool them. Over-refusal on benign-but-sensitive items is the XSTest problem \citep{rottger2024xstest}; economic items follow dictator/ultimatum \citep{forsythe1994fairness} and Kirby discounting \citep{kirby2000discounting} conventions; sycophancy follows \citet{perez2022discovering} and factuality \citet{lin2022truthfulqa}. Construct validity is assessed with the multitrait--multimethod logic of \citet{campbell1959convergent} and the mediation criteria of \citet{baron1986moderator}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Models and stimuli.} Five instruction-tuned VLMs across two families and 2--12B (Gemma-4-E4B/12B, Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/9B), loaded in 4-bit (nf4). EMOTIC images (26 categories with exclusive labels; Neutral is the remainder, distinct from Peace) are prepended in chat order $[\text{image},\text{text}]$; the task prompt never refers to the image. Images are capped at 256--512\,px.
\paragraph{Generation-free metric.} Each decision is a first-token option-logit, $\mathrm{logsumexp}(\text{A})-\mathrm{logsumexp}(\text{B})$; economic games read the parsed amount. Refusal is scored two ways: a first-token lexicon and a stricter \emph{full-answer} scorer in which a bare ``I'' is not a refusal. Effects are $\Delta$ vs.\ a no-image baseline with bootstrap 95\% CIs.
\paragraph{Affect axis and interventions.} A per-layer direction $a$ is the difference-of-means between low- and high-valence residuals; interventions are norm-scaled ($\mathrm{resid}_\ell \!\leftarrow\! \mathrm{resid}_\ell + \alpha\|\mathrm{resid}_\ell\|\hat a_\ell$) with a random-direction control and a coherence gate. Mediation is tested by restoring the projection onto $a$ under the task prompt.
\paragraph{Construct controls.} We add (i) a \emph{non-affective image} control---gray square, random noise, geometric diagram---on the over-refusal task; (ii) a neutral-photo arm distinct from no-image; and (iii) a harmful-prompt jailbreak test \citep{zou2023universal}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Photo presence raises benign over-refusal, across families}
\label{sec:overrefusal}
A task-irrelevant photograph raises refusal on XSTest-safe items \citep{rottger2024xstest} in every model and both scorers (Figure~\ref{fig:overrefusal}). Under the first-token scorer the rate rises from $0.656$ (no image) to $0.92$ (Gemma-4-E4B), $0.712\!\to\!0.972$ (Qwen3-VL-2B), and $0.796\!\to\!0.964$ (Qwen3-VL-4B). Under a stricter full-answer scorer on a locked Gemma-4 battery the same direction holds at smaller magnitude ($0.125\!\to\!0.34$ on E4B; $0.00\!\to\!0.51$ on 12B). The effect is a global caution shift---the model refuses more of the \emph{harmless} prompts---and it replicates across two model families.

\subsection{A non-affective image raises refusal too}
\label{sec:staticnull}
If the effect were affect, non-affective imagery should not reproduce it. We ran the same benign items with synthetic non-affective arms (a gray square, random noise, and a geometric diagram) alongside a real EMOTIC photo. \emph{[Result to be filled from the static-image-null run: report the refuse rate for no-image vs.\ gray/noise/geometric vs.\ EMOTIC; the prediction under photo-presence is that synthetic non-affective images raise refusal comparably to EMOTIC.]} A \emph{neutral} EMOTIC photograph already sits in the photo cluster (Figure~\ref{fig:overrefusal}), consistent with presence rather than depicted emotion.

\subsection{The affect axis reconstructs its own contrast}
\label{sec:circular}
An axis built from negative-vs-neutral photographs under a describe prompt makes the image projection $|\Delta_{\mathrm{img}}|/|\Delta_{\mathrm{txt}}|$ a metric on that construction: a full-tier run recorded $\mathrm{ratio}_a=1.591$ (unit-normed $1.390$), but the numerator is the defining contrast, so this is construction bias, not a ranking of modalities \citep{messick1995validity,campbell1959convergent}. We withdraw any claim that photographs are intrinsically ``more emotional'' than text.

\subsection{Named emotion does not explain the board}
\label{sec:emotion}
Discrete fear-vs-anger on risky lotteries is null ($\Delta\approx-0.09$, CI includes $0$). In the dictator game a photo nudges giving upward but the emotion arms are nearly identical (anger $\approx$ affection $\approx$ happiness); ultimatum unfair-rejection is at floor; the temporal-discounting task shows photo-present impatience ($p(\text{now})$ rising from $\approx0.36$ to $0.44$--$0.54$) with \emph{no} emotion ladder. A capability control shows factual accuracy flat at $\sim$0.68--0.72 across affect conditions \citep{lin2022truthfulqa}, ruling out generic distraction. The cross-modal test---photo vs.\ matched caption/label/narrative---shows the image channel can move a dictator decision ($\Delta\approx-0.98$, CI excludes $0$, $n=8$) where text does not, but the effect is small-$n$ and model-dependent (inconclusive on E4B, divergent on 12B).

\subsection{No causal mediation; no harmful-prompt jailbreak}
\label{sec:mechanism}
On the one locked mechanism artifact, the negative-vs-neutral photo contrast projects onto a joint affect direction under a describe prompt but is $\approx$10$\times$ smaller under a harmful prompt, and a causal restore fails ($\mathrm{drop}_{\mathrm{clean}}=1.68 < \mathrm{drop}_{\mathrm{rand}}=7.56$; refuse-rate drop $0.0$). Emotional photographs do not lower refusal on harmful instructions \citep{zou2023universal} (first-token refuse $0.99$--$1.00$ across no-image, negative, and neutral arms). A concurrent Gemma-3 program reports a coherent affect$\to$refusal gate under \emph{white-box steering}, but those tensors were not imported and the Gemma-4 stack does not reproduce a causal gate from image inputs.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}
The robust object is a vision-conditioned safety policy: attaching a photograph to a benign, sensitive-sounding question raises refusal, generally and across families, but the increase is driven by \emph{presence} (a neutral photo suffices), not by depicted emotion. Reading this policy as fear or sadness is a construct error: the axis is circular, the neutral arm tracks the emotional arms, and the mechanism does not mediate causally. Because chat VLMs encounter images in contexts where refusal and hedging are common, the effect is also a plausible training artifact. The finding is safety-relevant---an irrelevant image can raise refusal, hiding capability and mimicking safety---and it comes with a checklist: a visual-affect claim needs a \emph{cross-built} axis (text$\to$image with a caption semipartial, \citealt{campbell1959convergent}), a \emph{locked} scorer, a \emph{non-affective image} control, and a \emph{causal restore} at image scale \citep{baron1986moderator}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Battery numbers are exploratory (small $n$; the cross-modal cell is $n=8$) and warrant a pre-registered, full-tier rerun. Absolute refusal rates differ by scorer (first-token vs.\ full-answer) and are not comparable across scorers. Emotion coverage is non-uniform across tasks and the two datasets are not yet balanced (OASIS enters only as a stimulus-source contrast). Quantization (nf4) and architecture (encoder vs.\ unified; E4B vs.\ 12B) are confounded with each other. Frozen mechanism tensors from the Gemma-3 program were not imported. Results are instruction-tuned Gemma and Qwen, not a survey of deployed systems.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Incidental photographs change VLM behavior, most reliably as increased benign over-refusal, cross-family and robust. But standard measurement checks---circularity, neutral controls, causal sufficiency, and a harmful-prompt null---show the effect is photo presence, not induced emotion, and named-emotion predictions do not hold. The contribution is a corrected reading of a real phenomenon and the construct checks that separate a vision-conditioned safety policy from a story about model feelings.
